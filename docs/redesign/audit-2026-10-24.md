# Player audit, rules 3.6.0 (2026-10-24)

Written 2026-10-24 on `patch-6` at `8c1a586`. This audit changes no code. It reports what a player meets, and what different ways of playing lead to, so that Rich can choose what comes next. Under the blueprint's working rules, every design point below is an option for Rich to decide; nothing here is decided.

## How it was played

- **In the app.** I ran the development build against three scratch saves made by `scripts/screens-data.ts`, each with its own data and browser storage, so the live campaign was never read or touched. The saves were a week-5 campaign (about where the live one is), a 16-week campaign played by the test driver, and a 20-day absence. I drove them with real clicks: every tab and the How to play guide. On the week-5 save I bought four Tier IIs, Castle II and two Crossings, laid a siege, fought both daily battles by hand, sealed a 14-day contract and advanced the clock. On the 16-week save I opened an audience, laid a siege from the hex panel, fought a Mythic Hunt by hand, and loaded the Ultimatum and Victory scenarios. On the absence save I read the Homecoming.
- **Headless.** I played 14 styles of player for 76 weeks with 4 seeds each, using the game's own `autoplay`, `settle` and the simulator's ledgers. That is 56 whole campaigns. The styles were the simulator's greedy and smarter players, plus seven new ones:
  - **turtle**: never assaults a hex; it gains land only by courting villages and bribes.
  - **weekly** and **monthly**: open the game side only on the first day of each week or of each 4-week month. They still log their health every day.
  - **away3w**: logs and plays nothing for weeks 12 to 14.
  - **led**: fights every daily battle with the Marshal's play.
  - **armFirst**: spends on the Armory before buildings.
  - **diplomat**: takes land by deals.

  The Perfect, Committed, Wavering, Casual and long-plateau profiles were played the smarter way. The script lives in the session scratchpad, not the repo.

## What works (keep it)

- **Absence is forgiving.** Three weeks fully away cost 1 to 3 weeks of victory. The Homecoming is kind and clear: what was settled, what was held, the purse, the Healer's check-in and a short contract to start again.
- **Behavior leads, weight doesn't punish.** A long plateau from week 9 wins in the same weeks as Steadfast, which suits a weight journey.
- **Something to buy almost every week.** The smarter player buys a tier, Crossing, Elite or item in 43 of its first 60 weeks.
- **Live feedback.** Raising a tier immediately moved today's threat from "matched" to "weaker", and the estimate under the orders updates as you change them. The contract page shows what each score would pay before you seal.
- **The daily battle and the Grand Battle screens.** Both are easy to read and quick to play: two rounds of orders, a swap, and a clear result with what it posts.
- **The Audience** says why a deal is out of reach ("needs Respect 60"). **How each rival works** sets out Respect against Renown well.

## A. Design questions (Rich's call)

### A1. The war barely decides the victory

**What happens.** Victory week is set by the pacing rule (D-41: none before week 16, then one rival every 12 weeks) and by habits, since Accords run on Realm Consistency.
- The full player's four resolutions land exactly 12 weeks apart. After the first one, the remaining 36 weeks are on rails: the next rival is ready before its slot opens.
- A turtle that never assaults a hex wins 4 weeks later.
- A player who opens the game side once a month, holding 24 hexes at week 52, wins at weeks 63 to 74.
- Leading every battle by hand, arming first, or dealing instead of fighting won no sooner.

**Why it matters.** The war is where the daily choices are (which siege, which companies, which order), yet those choices don't move the one outcome that ends the game. A player who works out the turtle path loses the reason to fight. Seen the other way, it is a strength: habits win the game and the war is the fun. That fits Pillar 1 and keeps a bad war from costing a good health journey.

**Options.**
1. **Keep it, and say so.** The war is for Renown, perks, land and the story; habits set the pace. Add the road-to-victory strip (C4) so the pacing is visible rather than discovered. Cheapest; no rule change.
2. **Let the war buy time.** For example, a resolution held by pacing comes 1 week sooner for each path rank above II on that rival's path, down to a floor of 8 weeks. Deeds then shorten the wait without letting a sprint end the journey early (R-17). This changes numbers, so it would need the simulator against G-11.
3. **Let the war be needed.** Accord talks or terms also need some Dominion on that rival's path, so a turtle can't Accord its way through. This tightens G-18 and G-07 and risks walling off weaker players.

**Recommendation: 1 now, 2 if the war still feels optional after a few weeks of play.**

### A2. A good player loses most of their defenses in weeks 9 to 20

**What happens.** For the smarter Steadfast player (93% of duties kept), 22% of defenses are lost in month 1 and 38% in month 2. In months 3 to 5 (weeks 9 to 20) it is 70 to 75%, about 5 to 6 lost battles a week, then mostly around half through month 14. Beasts and mythics cause most of them (57% in months 3 to 5, more later; raids most of the rest), striking deep march hexes at those hexes' own wild strength (D-15). 48 of the 49 hexes the player lost in the sampled campaign went back to the wilds. The losses come with the frontier, not with behavior: the turtle loses 21% and the monthly player 1%, and G-08 already finds bad weeks lose only 1.37× as much land as good ones. The live campaign is at week 4 and about to enter this stretch.

**Why it matters.** Rich's rules for the game are that rewards land fast and rest feels good. Here a player keeping their habits reads "lost" several times a week, and better habits can't prevent it. A loss tied to nothing the player did is noise, and it works against reinforcement.

**Options.**
1. **Tie it to behavior.** Beasts and mythics strike deep hexes at their wild strength scaled by the defense's Valor, so a good day makes them weaker. This mends G-08 at the same time.
2. **Soften the frontier.** Beasts strike a hex the player holds at, say, 75% of its wild strength while its march's preparation is low. Or lower the defense's losses without touching the strike: a beaten defense on a march hex costs tribute but keeps the hex once in two times.
3. **Keep the losses, change how they read.** "The frontier gave ground at X" in place of "lost", and the Homecoming and tidings count hexes held rather than battles lost. Words only; text still waits on A3.

**Recommendation: 1.** It is the only option that makes a defense something the player's habits win.

### A3. The Steward asks for firmer terms, but firmer terms only cost

After 4 weeks above 95% the Steward suggests a firmer Charter. Every score is a share of the Charter, clamped at 100%. A larger step pool or a tighter calorie limit therefore lowers pay, Valor and Accord Respect, with nothing to make up for it, while a soft Charter scores 100% forever.

**Options:** (1) a one-time reward for raising the Charter, such as Renown, reputation or a Momentum boost; (2) pay scaled by the Charter's ambition against the founding Charter; (3) leave the game neutral and word the Steward's counsel as health advice, not a game move.

**Recommendation: 1.** It is the smallest change that stops punishing a player for taking the advice.

### A4. The Ultimatum is near-certain and never lethal

Earlier simulator runs (G-19 at 3.6.0) put an Ultimatum in 75% of Steadfast campaigns and 96 to 100% of Committed and Wavering ones, and falls at 0%. It is the most alarming card in the game, and the player is given no idea what to do about it (C6). Either keep it as a dramatic beat and tell the player how to answer it, or let it fire only when the player can actually fall.

### A5. The late game

- **The best content arrives too late to use.** The smarter player buys Tier V in weeks 54 to 59 and Castle IV in week 60, right at victory.
- **The purse piles up.** Players who win later pile up 8 to 16k: Perfect before week 53, Committed before week 66. The greedy player runs out of things to buy at week 64 and then banks about 1,700 a week.
- **The Reign is empty.** It reads "the realm goes on" but has no goals.

Weight maintenance after the goal is the hardest part of a journey, so a Reign built around keeping Milestones (A-116) could be the strongest long-term feature. That is blueprint open question 8, and worth scheduling.

## B. Bugs and traps (no rule change; safe for the live save)

1. **The Assault dropdown cancels today's assault.** Choosing a target from the dropdown (`withTarget`, `view/orders.ts`) sets the standing siege but sends no companies. It also writes the day's own orders, so the Marshal doesn't press the siege today: "Today's orders don't press it." **Lay siege** on the hex panel (`laySiege`, `combat.ts`) picks the companies. A player who picks from the dropdown and walks away gets no assault that day. The fix: make the dropdown pick the companies as Lay siege does when none are assigned.
2. **The Herald's "Grand Battles" heading holds other warnings.** The `warning` section (`Herald.tsx`) also carries tomorrow's foreseen surge, Goblin buy-backs, Goblin bids and uprisings. The fix: rename it "Coming up" or "Warnings".
3. **Dates print in ISO format.** 45 text templates take a date fact, and it comes through raw: "Scorched until 2026-10-21", "the Siege of the Crown comes on 2027-02-11". The fix: format ISO dates in `t()`'s facts, for example "Thu, Feb 11". This is fact formatting, not story text.
4. **Battle orders that can't do anything.** The round's hand is a slice of a deck shuffled at setup with no check that an order applies (`offeredOn`, `grand/field.ts`). Seen in play: Arcane Ward against beasts that cast nothing, Bribe ("one non-mythic enemy") against a host of mythic Griffins, and Foresight ("see the next round's intents") in the last round. The fix: leave out orders that can't apply when the deck is built; the deck is saved with the battle, so existing battles are untouched.
5. **A Truce or pact offered at Peace.** At Peace there are already no raids or conquest attempts. A Truce (150 at week 5) then buys only +2 Respect, and a pact (250) buys nothing, yet the Audience offers both with **Strike** (`land.ts` judges neither by the standing). The fix: refuse them at Peace, or say what they still add.
6. **"Rings" in player text.** The map now speaks of marches and depth, but some lines still say rings.
   - Shieldwall says "walls count double on rings 4 and 5", which means march hexes at depth 5 and deeper (`bandOf`).
   - The Homecoming says "Rings 0 to 2 held, as they always will", and a battle report says "Rings 0 to 2 cannot be taken".

   The fix: say "the heartland" and "depth 5 and deeper".
7. **The Archive contradicts itself in a campaign.** The campaign's contracts are listed at the top. Below them, the ledger's summary reads "Contracts closed 0 … Weight change ±0 lb" and "The shelves are bare." The fix: hide the ledger-only summary in a campaign, or count the campaign's contracts in it.
8. **Rumors never change.** Each rival's rumor is what his turn spent most on. Budgets split by fixed personality shares, so all four lines are the same every day: in both saves, at weeks 5 and 16, Ugrak is always "buying troops". The fix: rumor what changed this week (a new wall level, a Warhost fund past half), or show the rumors only at the week close.

## C. Clarity and polish

1. **Diplomacy repeats itself.** Each court repeats the front rules ("Each pair of neighboring rivals shares a front…" four times) and the at-war paragraph for every front (eight times). Keep a single line per front on the court, with the full rules in How each rival works.
2. **The target list doesn't say what can be taken.** At week 16 the Assault dropdown lists 36 hexes as "unaligned · garrison 95.1 · flanked +10%". Whether a siege falls today, or how long it would take, shows only after you pick one. Sort the list by "falls today / in N days / long siege" and show each hex's path and Dominion. Garrisons and Assault are already shown, so nothing hidden is revealed.
3. **The map shows too little at first, then too much.** At the opening zoom the map shows no threat, siege or stronghold marker ("zoom in for the details"). One step in, the terrain art is so busy that ownership and markers are hard to pick out. Show today's threat and the standing siege at every zoom, and draw ownership as a stronger tint or border.
4. **The pacing is invisible.** A blocked resolution says only "The realm cannot resolve another rival yet", with no week. A held resolution isn't shown at all. A small strip on Diplomacy would cover it, for example: "Rivals resolved 1 of 4 · the next can fall from week 34 · Hrodgar's Accord is ready and waits for it".
5. **Decimals everywhere.** Garrison 13.5, tribute 1.5, spoils 4.1, Respect +46.6, health 8.52/16.8. Round the figures on screen to whole numbers. The battle log's "12.43, dealt 9.94" needs a label (what was struck, and what got through).
6. **The Ultimatum card says nothing about what to do.** Add a line naming the levers, without numbers: "Beat his raids, take his land and win his battles before the week closes."
7. **The top bar's "PURSE · 96% · GRACE II" is cryptic.** It explains itself only on hover. Name it, for example "Consistency 96%".
8. **The Marshal leaves ranged companies on the bench (minor).** His Grand Battle pick is the top companies by power and matchup, placed afterwards. In the Mythic Hunt he set a melee Clay Golems in a rear slot, where melee does nothing until a front falls, and left two ranged companies of the same power out. Choosing for the slots would fix it, but it is also a place where a player's own formation beats his, which may be worth keeping.

## Suggested order

1. **A polish patch, B1 to B8 and C1 to C7.** No rule change, so the live campaign carries forward with no migration. B1, B3 and B4 matter most in daily play.
2. **A design session on A1 and A2**, with simulator runs for whichever options Rich picks. A2 is timely: the live campaign reaches weeks 9 to 20 next.
3. **A3 to A5** when the late game is in view.
